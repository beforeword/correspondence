Subject: Common ground: the grounds for attributing mutual knowledge

Dear Professor Tomasello,

Your 2020 paper with Wouter Wolf on video watching and common ground (JECP, 199, 104930) describes common ground in terms of participants who “know that they both know” they are experiencing something together.

The study varies the timing of eye contact, reports differences in children’s subsequent approach times, and discusses methodological limitations.

Please indicate the strongest ground, in the reported experimental comparison, for attributing to the children an understanding that both participants knew they were watching together.

beforeword proposes keeping three elements separately inspectable: the written report, the attribution of mutual knowledge, and the grounds offered for accepting that attribution.

The terms “knowledge” and “common ground” remain part of the writing under examination. Written descriptions of methods, measurement records, criteria and their justifications, human self-descriptions, and this letter receive the same treatment.
