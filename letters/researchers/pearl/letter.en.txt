Subject: Causal models: the grounds for interpreting evidence

Dear Professor Pearl,

In Section 3.1 of “Causal inference in statistics: An overview”, you write: “In interpreting this equation one should think of a physical process”.

Your discussion explicitly distinguishes the algebraic equation from its causal interpretation.

Please indicate whether your framework represents the grounds for reading an experimental report as support for a causal assumption, or treats that justification as separate from the causal model.

beforeword proposes keeping three elements separately inspectable: the written record, its selected interpretation, and the grounds offered for accepting that interpretation. This examination includes the records offered as supporting evidence.

The phrase “physical process” also remains part of the writing under examination. Written accounts of experiments, expert judgments, and any proposed criterion receive the same treatment. This letter is included.
