## Why this letter was written

*beforeword’s explanation for readers; separate from the sent letter.*

**Starting point.** The letter addresses Williamson’s quoted thesis about knowledge as evidence. It asks how a written supporting statement justifies saying that a reader knows a particular claim is true.

**Why it matters.** This matters when assessing an argument: an added explanation has its own wording, interpretation and claimed role in supporting a conclusion.

**What the letter asks.** The letter asks for one sentence, a proposed reading and a supporting statement, followed by an account of how that support warrants attributing knowledge to the reader. Calling the statement a justification is also open to examination.

**Subject: Knowledge as Evidence: one sentence, one supporting claim**

---

Dear Professor Williamson,

In “Knowledge as Evidence”, you write: “knowledge, and only knowledge, constitutes evidence”.

The reading proposed here treats E = K as a substantive thesis and includes your rejection of an infallible test for identifying evidence.

A written explanation adds another formulation. beforeword asks that its interpretation and its role in supporting the original claim be made explicit. The label “justification” is itself part of what is being examined.

Please indicate, using one sentence from this letter, a proposed reading and one supporting statement, explaining how that statement would support attributing to a reader knowledge that the claim, so interpreted, is true.

The supporting statement would extend the written record. Its wording, its interpretation, and its proposed evidential role would remain separately inspectable.

This examination also includes any stated criteria, descriptions of observations, human self-descriptions, and the wording of beforeword’s own procedure. This letter and its terms “writing”, “reading”, “knowledge” and “evidence” receive the same treatment.
