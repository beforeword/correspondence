## Why this letter was written

*beforeword’s explanation for readers; separate from the sent letter.*

**Starting point.** The letter examines a step in the discussion of Scientist AI: reading “S asserted X” as a record of an observed act of communication and using that reading as a premise.

**Why it matters.** Readers need to distinguish the written sentence, the event it is taken to record, and the reason for accepting that reading in AI training. The label “observation”, source information and explanations of their role remain records that can themselves be examined.

**What the letter asks.** The letter asks for the criterion used to accept such a description as an observation for training, and how the reasons for applying it can remain open to examination.

**Subject: Scientist AI: the grounds for treating a record as an observation**

---

Dear Professor Bengio,

“S asserted X” first appears as a written form. Reading it as a record of an observed communication act assigns it a role that requires grounds.

Section 3.2.1 of “Safety from Honesty in a Disinterested AI Predictor” provides a concrete place to examine this step. Remark 3.27 already acknowledges possible contextualization errors.

beforeword proposes keeping three things separately inspectable: what is written, what it is read as recording, and the grounds for using that reading as a premise.

This applies equally to the framework’s own formulations. Describing something as “observed” is itself a written attribution; the grounds for admitting it as an observation need to remain explicit. Source metadata and explanations of those grounds remain further records, subject to the same examination.

The argument and examples:  
https://beforeword.xyz/research/no-authority-in-the-output/en/

Please consider a brief response on the criterion your framework uses to admit such an attribution as an observation for training, and how the grounds for applying that criterion would remain available for examination.

Kirill Shebetov  
beforeword

\+

---
