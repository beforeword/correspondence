## Why this letter was written

*beforeword’s explanation for readers; separate from the sent letter.*

**Starting point.** The letter responds to a passage in Hinton’s On Point interview transcript where a reformulation is said to mean the same thing as a sentence using “subjective experience”.

**Why it matters.** Readers need to see what supports the claim that a change in wording preserves meaning, especially when that claim supports a further conclusion about subjective experience. The proposal keeps the sentences and the claim of equivalence open to separate examination.

**What the letter asks.** Using the robot-and-prism passage, the letter asks for a criterion for that equivalence and a reason for applying it in this case.

**Subject: Your account of subjective experience: “the same thing”**

---

Dear Professor Hinton,

In the published transcript of your On Point interview of 10 January 2025, a reformulation of a sentence using “subjective experience” is presented as one that “says the same thing”.

beforeword proposes examining the grounds for that claim.  
The two sentences appear as written forms. Reading them as equivalent adds an interpretation of their relation. That interpretation does not, by itself, establish equivalence.

The robot-and-prism passage provides a concrete place to examine the move from wording to an attribution of subjective experience. The proposal keeps the wording, the selected interpretation, and the stated grounds for using it in a further conclusion separately inspectable.

This applies equally to human self-descriptions and to this letter. Stated grounds remain further writing, available for the same examination.  
The reading procedure and worked examples:  
https://beforeword.xyz/model/en/

Please indicate the criterion for treating the reformulation as equivalent, and the grounds for applying it here.

Kirill Shebetov  
beforeword

\+

---
