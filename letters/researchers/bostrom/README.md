## Why this letter was written

*beforeword’s explanation for readers; separate from the sent letter.*

**Starting point.** The letter takes up a virtual-animal example described in Bostrom and Shulman’s work. It focuses on the move from a described response by the virtual animal to a claim that the system experiences pain.

**Why it matters.** Readers need to distinguish the described behavior, its satisfaction of a chosen criterion, and the claim about experience. The connection between them should be open to examination.

**What the letter asks.** The letter asks for one premise supporting that move and an explanation of its role. The same examination applies to the criterion itself, human self-reports, and the wording of the letter.

**Subject: Virtual nociceptors: grounds for attributing pain**

---

Dear Dr Bostrom,

In “Propositions Concerning Digital Minds and Society” (v. 1.21), with Carl Shulman, the virtual-animal example includes the phrase “behavioral criteria used to identify animal pain”.

The reading proposed here preserves the draft’s tentative status and its qualification that behavioural criteria may be met while key internal features are missing.

For the described avoidance of virtual nociceptor activation, please indicate one supporting premise and explain how it would support an attribution of experienced pain to the system.

beforeword proposes keeping the written scenario, the proposed experiential interpretation, and the supporting premises separately inspectable.

A written explanation adds another formulation. Its interpretation and its proposed evidential role also enter the examination; the label “evidence” leaves that role to be shown.

The same examination includes descriptions of algorithms and animal behaviour, human self-reports, stated criteria, the wording of beforeword’s own procedure, and this letter—including its terms “pain”, “evidence”, “writing” and “reading”.

The reading procedure and worked examples:  
https://beforeword.xyz/model/en/

Kirill Shebetov  
beforeword
