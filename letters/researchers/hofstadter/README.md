## Why this letter was written

*beforeword’s explanation for readers; separate from the sent letter.*

**Starting point.** The letter considers a conditional argument: if a soul is defined as a pattern, a sufficiently detailed reproduction of that pattern counts, under the definition, as the soul itself.

**Why it matters.** Readers need to see what is being compared and how that comparison supports treating two instances of the pattern as the same self.

**What the letter asks.** The letter asks what level of detail counts as sufficient and what supports applying that criterion in a particular case. The definition and the wording used to explain it also remain open to examination.

**Subject: Your account of the self: from definition to attribution**

---

Dear Professor Hofstadter,

In your September 2007 interview with Tal Cohen and Yarden Nir-Buchbinder, you discuss a conditional claim: if a soul is a pattern, a sufficiently fine-grained realization of that pattern counts, under your definition, as “the soul itself”.

Please indicate the criterion of sufficient detail for treating two proposed realizations as the same self, and the grounds for applying it in a particular case.

beforeword proposes keeping three elements separately inspectable: the written comparison, the attribution of a shared identity, and the grounds offered for that attribution.

The terms used to explain the attribution—including “pattern” and “self”—remain part of the writing under examination. Definitions and their written justifications receive the same treatment. This also applies to human self-descriptions and to this letter.
