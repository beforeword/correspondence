## Why this letter was written

*beforeword’s explanation for readers; separate from the sent letter.*

**Starting point.** The letter responds to a quoted statement that the brain constructs reality from bits of incoming information. It asks what supports accepting that sentence as an explanation of perception.

**Why it matters.** The description addresses the reader directly: “Your brain”. This makes it important to show what warrants reading those words as an explanation of the reader’s own perception.

**What the letter asks.** The letter asks for a criterion for accepting the explanation and a reason for applying it here. The criterion and its written justification remain open to examination.

**Subject: Your account of perception: the grounds for accepting it**

---

Dear Wojciech,

On your website, under “Understanding Yourself”, you write: “Your brain constructs reality from bits of incoming information.”

Please indicate a criterion for accepting this formulation as an explanation of perception, and the grounds for applying that criterion.

beforeword proposes keeping three elements separately inspectable: the written formulation, the interpretation assigned to it, and the grounds offered for accepting that interpretation.

The terms “brain” and “reality” remain part of the wording under examination. The criterion, its written justification, human self-descriptions, and this letter receive the same treatment.
