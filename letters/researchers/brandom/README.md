## Why this letter was written

*beforeword’s explanation for readers; separate from the sent letter.*

**Starting point.** The letter examines a quoted account in which making an assertion commits its author to justify it if suitably challenged.

**Why it matters.** For readers, the issue is what commitment others infer from their words and why that particular reading should apply to them.

**What the letter asks.** The letter asks for one of its own sentences to be used to show what warrants attributing such a commitment to its writer. Accounts of the social practices offered to explain that attribution are also open to examination.

**Subject: A sentence in this letter: grounds for attributing commitment**

---

Dear Professor Brandom,

In “Conceptual Content and Discursive Practice”, you write that, in asserting a claim, “one is committing oneself to justify the claim if suitably challenged”.

Your account locates that responsibility within social practice.

Please indicate, using one sentence from this letter, what would warrant attributing an assertional commitment to its writer.

beforeword proposes keeping three elements separately inspectable: the written sentence, the commitment attributed on reading it, and the grounds offered for that attribution.

The terms “commitment” and “entitlement” remain part of the writing under examination. Descriptions of social practices, any criterion offered and its written justification, human self-descriptions, and this letter receive the same treatment.
