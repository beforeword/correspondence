## Why this letter was written

*beforeword’s explanation for readers; separate from the sent letter.*

**Starting point.** The letter cites a passage about a new word coming to evoke the same idea in different people. It asks about the move from two written responses to a conclusion that both people had the same idea.

**Why it matters.** Readers need to see what in the two responses supports the claim that the word evoked the same idea for both people.

**What the letter asks.** The letter asks for a criterion for that conclusion and an explanation of its application. The responses, the conclusion about a shared idea and the reasons supporting it remain separate parts of the examination.

**Subject: The same idea: the grounds for that attribution**

---

Dear Professor Pinker,

In Chapter 1 of The Stuff of Thought, you describe how a new word “comes to evoke the same idea in the rest of a population”.

Please indicate a criterion for reading two people’s written responses as evidence that a word evoked the same idea for both, and the grounds for applying that criterion.

Your distinction between language and thought provides a starting point for this examination.

beforeword proposes keeping three elements separately inspectable: the written responses, the attribution of a shared idea, and the grounds offered for accepting that attribution.

The phrase “the same idea” remains part of the writing under examination. The criterion, its written justification, human self-descriptions, and this letter receive the same treatment.
