## Why this letter was written

*beforeword’s explanation for readers; separate from the sent letter.*

**Starting point.** The letter starts from the clarification of “Talking About Large Language Models”, which connects discussion of belief to the use of words and to the capabilities and behavior of the system being described.

**Why it matters.** The same question arises when a person writes “I believe this”: identical words may be read differently depending on their stated source. If that difference supports a conclusion about a person or a model, the proposal asks for the reason to be made explicit.

**What the letter asks.** The letter asks whether applying this requirement to people helps clarify talk of belief, or demands more justification than ordinary language use requires.

**Subject: The human side of your account of LLM belief**

---

Dear Murray,

Your clarification of “Talking About Large Language Models” places the discussion of belief in the use of words, with attention to the capabilities and behaviour of the system being described.

beforeword proposes a requirement for explanations that rely on such attributions: keep the recorded words, the interpretation added to them, and the grounds for that interpretation separately inspectable. This includes human self-descriptions.

For “I believe this”, a human source may warrant a different interpretation from an AI source. The proposed requirement is to make the grounds for that difference explicit wherever the attribution supports a further conclusion.

The reading procedure and worked examples:  
https://beforeword.xyz/model/en/

Please consider a brief critical response on the human case—particularly whether this requirement clarifies belief-attribution or asks for more justification than ordinary usage requires.

Kirill Shebetov  
beforeword

\+

---
